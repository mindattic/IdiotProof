# Recent runners, 2026-09-10 .. 2026-11-09: case studies

**Case study (opened window, not a test).** IP-T486, under [amendment 05](amendment-05.md). Nothing here tests a
hypothesis or changes a frozen verdict. Any rule this suggests must be tested on train (ledger-counted), then on
validation or the remaining holdout through a registered amendment, and proven forward in the 20-day paper shadow.
It is never tested on this window. The locked holdout (2025-11-03 .. 2026-09-09) was not read: no price, volume, label or
ranking dated inside it was requested or computed.

The question: *what news, filings, announcements or earnings make a stock that was nothing suddenly worth 300% more,
and then die?* The method: take the five biggest intraday runners of every session in the last two months, and study
each one directly.

Data: [`case-studies-2026-09-10_2026-11-09.json`](case-studies-2026-09-10_2026-11-09.json) has one record per stock-day,
with every news item, filing and the R4 check. The generated tables and all 210 dossiers are in
[`runs/case-studies-2026-09-10_2026-11-09.generated.md`](runs/case-studies-2026-09-10_2026-11-09.generated.md).

## Read this first: what this data is

The numbers come from the `casestudy` run that finished at **2026-11-09 15:16 ET**. The code is
`tools/GapperStudy/CaseStudy.cs` at the state of that run. The planned rerun after the close was stopped by the host
because the machine was low on memory. Per the brief, it was not retried. As a result, these results have known limits:

- **2026-11-09 is a partial session.** Close and fade are as of about 15:02 ET. The same applies to D+1 for 11-06 rows
  and D+2 for 11-05 rows.
- **11-09 prior closes drift by a few percent.** The raw and split-adjusted pulls ran minutes apart while the market was
  live. The adjustment ratio is now taken from the opens. For OMH the true prior close is $7.36 (the 11-06 close), but
  the data says $7.55.
- **Halts are missing.** The Nasdaq halt feed pads its times (`06:55:00 .000`), and `HaltHistory` dropped every halt in
  the window. That parser is fixed in the code commit.
- **About 1 in 4 catalysts are unresolved filings.** The classifier in this run did not yet read the exhibit-99 press
  release of a 6-K or 8-K. 49 stock-days, 34 of them foreign filers, show "other company news", most of them from a bare filing.
  CKX on 11-09 is labelled "reverse split" when the catalyst is the $1B Jacobs investment. The code now reads exhibit
  text and fixes CKX, but the stored data is from before that change.
- **Named stock-days outside the top 5 are not in the data.** These are GFR, TLS, FRVO, PCT and STAK on 11-09. They come
  from a trial run at about 15:29 ET and are quoted in the today section only.

To refresh everything after any close, run:

```
GAPPER_STUDY_DIR=docs\research\gapper-study-2026-11 GapperStudy casestudy --from 2026-09-10 --to 2026-11-09 --top 5 \
  --extra "2026-11-09:CKX,GFR,TLS,FRVO,STAK,MF,PCT,WCT,NWGL;2026-11-06:NWGL;2026-11-04:NWGL" --user <owner>
```

The run takes about 25 minutes and needs about 750 Alpaca requests and 650 EDGAR requests.

## Answer in one paragraph

The typical recent +300% runner had no rated catalyst. It was a sub-$3, often foreign-filed (Cayman, China, Hong
Kong or Singapore) name with a float of a few million shares or less, and frequently a reverse split in the last two
months. It had a dilution filing on record and had already been a top mover in the past month. It ignited on volume,
usually premarket, peaked about two hours later, and gave back three quarters of the move within two sessions.

The real company events did run: an acquisition target, a $1B outside investment, an FDA approval, an earnings beat.
They were a minority, though, and they ran less far than the no-news and post-split squeezes. The exception is the
fake-merger and LOI type.

OMH is the purest example: 1:50 reverse split, a $40M shelf takedown on file, no news on either run day, +524% one day
and +1,068% two sessions later.

## OMH, worked in full

**Profile.** Ohmyhome Limited (OMH) is a Singapore-based foreign filer.

- A 1:50 reverse split took effect on **2026-08-31**. It was announced 2026-08-27 10:20 ET, 4 days ahead (Alpaca news).
- A **424B5 was filed 2026-10-07**: the $40M offering prospectus with Magnus Capital.
- The last XBRL cover count, divided by 50, gives about 445k shares outstanding. That is a stale proxy: shares sold
  under the 424B5 are not in it, but the float was clearly tiny.
- ADV over the window was about **$213k**. It had no top-mover day in the prior 30 window sessions.
- Neither day had any Alpaca news item or EDGAR filing from D-2 to D. **Catalyst: none found.** The only "event" is the
  split two months earlier with the offering paper behind it.

**Day 1, 2026-11-04 (QQQ +1.0%).**

|---|---|
| premarket | dead: high $2.95 (+1%) at 08:43 on 2,045 shares |
| 09:30 | open $2.99 |
| **09:38** | **ignition**: the minute's volume was 19.6x the trailing 30-minute average, close $3.49, a new session high |
| 09:40 | first +20% touch (after R4's 06:00-09:10 window) |

| 16:00 | close $15.55 (+433%) on **$652M** traded, about 3,000x its ADV |

On the way up it never lost VWAP and never pulled back 10% after the high. It ran into the close: a trend day with the
top in after-hours, not an opening spike.

**Fade.**

- D+1, 11-05: high $16.75, a lower high, on a day QQQ fell 6.5%. Close $13.35, -29% from the high.
- D+2, 11-06: close **$7.36**, -61% from the high. It gave back 72% of the run within two sessions.

**Day 2, 2026-11-09 (QQQ +3.5%), partial session.**

| time ET | what happened |
|---|---|
| 06:00 | already +105% over $7.36 |
| **07:55** | **ignition** at 15.4x volume, $17.00 |

R4 could not see OMH either day:

- On 11-04 it was flat (-3% at 06:00) until after R4's window closed. Its ADV was also under the $300k universe floor,
  so it was in the scan only because this check forced it in.
- On 11-09 it was +105% at 06:00, far above R4's 5-15% gap band.

**What OMH says.** The ingredients were all public and dated: the reverse split, the shelf takedown, a float well under
1M shares, and foreign registration. None of them was a news trigger. The trigger was the volume itself: a 20x minute
on day 1 and a 15x minute on day 2. The first run ended near the close. The second ended late in the morning, after
roughly 4.5 hours of fuel. The day-1 fade took it back to where day 2 started, which set up the second run.

## Recurring patterns, with counts (210 ranked stock-days, 164 names, 42 sessions)

1. **Most runners had no rated catalyst.**
   - 60 had no company news or filing at all from D-2 to D (29%).
   - Another 49 had only a filing or a vague item (23%). Of these, 25 filings were published after the prior close and
     were probably a press release on a 6-K or 8-K.
   - Rated real company events were 38 (18%).
   - "No catalyst" is not market beta here. Those 60 days split 28 up-QQQ and 32 down-QQQ. Five of the 11-05 top five
     ran on a -6.5% Nasdaq day.
2. **The splitters.**
   - 55 of 210 (26%) ran within 60 sessions of a reverse split or ADS ratio change. The median ratio was 1:12, the
     largest 1:200 (INLF).
   - 17 ran 0-10 sessions after one, and they had the highest median run, +206%.
   - In 42 of the 55, Alpaca news had announced the split ahead of the effective date (median 4.7 days).
3. **Dilution paper was nearly universal.**
   - 115 of 210 (55%) had an S-1, F-1, S-3, F-3, 424B or EFFECT filed in the prior 90 days.
   - 12 ran with an offering itself as the catalyst. PARA ran +428% after a 1:10 split and a fresh S-1, then closed -98%
     from the high by D+2.
   - 6 had an equity-line mention in news.
4. **The floats were tiny.**
   - 103 of the 172 with a usable XBRL count had under 10M shares outstanding (60%). 52 had under 2M.
   - The median is 5.5M. Shares are split-adjusted to D but stale after offerings, so they are a proxy only.
5. **Half were foreign filers.**
   - 101 of 210 (48%) file 20-F or 6-K, or have a non-US business address. 15 are ADR/ADS.
   - Foreign names ran more (median +158% vs +134%) and faded more (D+2 -50% vs -40% from the high).
6. **Repeat runners.**
   - 90 of 210 (43%) had already been a top-20 mover in the previous 30 window sessions.
   - 34 names appear on two or more days, 80 stock-days in all. Examples: VEEA and NCPL 4x; SDEV, AIXI and
     WORX 3-4x; OMH 2x.
7. **Ignition, mostly premarket, about 2 hours before the top.**
   - Premarket: 101 runners. Another 30 were already +20% at their first print.
   - 09:30-10:00: 28. 10:00-12:00: 34. 12:00-16:00: 11. After hours: 2. None found: 4.
   - The median from ignition to the high is 118 minutes.
   - 74 (35%) never got +20% premarket and made the whole move in regular hours, the OMH day-1 type.
8. **The high came later than in the 5-year data.**
   - High-of-day buckets: premarket 36 (17%), 09:30-10:00 34 (16%), 10:00-12:00 60 (29%), 12:00-16:00 53 (25%),
     after hours 27 (13%).
9. **How the top looked.**
   - 182 of 210 lost VWAP after the high, 106 of them within 30 minutes. The median is 22.5 minutes.
   - 178 pulled back 10%. 154 then made a failed lower-high retest (85-99% of the high).
   - Only 74 (35%) printed their biggest volume minute within 5 minutes of the high. The top is usually a
     lower-high-then-VWAP-loss sequence, not a volume climax.
   - Halts are not available in this data (see the limits above).
10. **They die by day 2.**
    - The median close is -33% from the high, D+1 -42%, D+2 -44%. 74% of the run is given back by D+2.
    - Only 27 of 205 made a higher high on D+1 (13%). 58 closed D+1 above D's close.
    - 42 of 200 were below the prior close by D+2: the entire run erased.
11. **Screener artifacts are common.** 61 stock-days in the window showed a raw gain of +50% or more that is under +20%
    split-adjusted:
    - NWGL 11-06: +8,676% raw, real +10%.
    - XXII 11-05: +5,233% raw, real -8%.
    - AIIO 11-06: +2,415% raw, real +1%.

## R4 (amendment 04): did it catch them, and how early?

R4 was swept 06:00-09:10 on a reconstructed morning cross-section every session. That cross-section is amendment
01's population rebuilt from window bars (candidates weight 1, seeded 10% sample weight 10, split-window days excluded)
plus the five runners forced in. It uses the frozen train profile and the shared `AlarmSweep` and `GapperAlarmData`
code, so this is the real composite rank, not a proxy.

**On the 210 runners:**

- 51 alarmed (24%).
- 21 alarmed before their first +20% touch (10%). The median lead was only **10 minutes**.
- 26 alarmed after the +20% touch, and 4 alarmed on mornings that never reached +20% by 09:45 (STAK 11-06, for
  example, alarmed at 08:26 and peaked at 16:03).
- Every alarm still came before the high of day. The median from alarm to high was 209 minutes.
- R4 is structurally blind to about a third of runners:
  - 60 were already +15% or more at 06:00, outside its [5%,15%) band.
  - 7 had no bars 04:00-09:45.
  - 90 had no alarm in the window, mostly because they ignited after 09:10.

**On the whole window population:** a weighted hit rate of 40.6% (244 of 601 forming mornings) at 1.79 false alarms per
100 scorable mornings. Train was 30.8% at 0.86, and validation was 36.1% at 1.56. So the alarm still finds forming
gappers, at a higher noise level.

**What this means:** R4 is a 06:00-09:10 gap-forming detector, not a runner detector. The recent big runners mostly
start too high (already gapped), or too late (regular-hours ignition), or from a float so small that ADV never put
them in the universe.

## What looks different from the 5-year train findings

The comparisons are with `runs/explore/watchlist-20261109-1945.md` (IP-T484, train runners of +100% or more),
`split-days-20261109-2016.md` (IP-T490) and the confirmatory report.

- **The high of day is later.** On train, 39% of +100% runners topped premarket and 13% at 09:30-10:00; the median
  high was 09:54. Recently only 17% topped premarket, 54% topped after 10:00, and 13% topped after the close.
- **More regular-hours-only runs.** "Under +20% premarket" was 22.7% of train runners and is 35% here.
- **Slower path.** Ignition to high is 118 minutes vs 96 on train. VWAP is lost later after the high (median 22.5 vs
  15 minutes).
- **The fade is the same.** The close is -33% vs -36% from the high on train. The "then die" part has not changed.
- **Post-split squeezes matter far more.**
  - On train a reverse-split day went +100% only 1.6-2.2% of the time (D through D+1). The median split-day run was
    +5-8%, with the high near 09:30.
  - Here 26% of all top runners were within 60 sessions of a split. The post-split ones ran the furthest, with highs
    several hours into the session.
  - The train split-days study also found no long ride that makes money (IP-T490). This window does not change that:
    post-split runners gave back 75% by D+2.
- **No-news and foreign names dominate.** On train, 24.8% of +100% runners had no same-day catalyst item; here 29% had
  none, and another 23% had only an unreadable filing. Half of the runners are foreign filers. This fits the
  promotion-ring pattern in `methods-who-buys.md` (small Asia-linked listings, about 14 SEC suspensions since 2025-09).
- **Repeat movers are the strongest prerequisite.** 43% of runners had a top-20 day in the prior 30 sessions. Train
  found a +100% day in the last 10 sessions gave 25x lift, and 7.1% ran +100% again within 5 sessions.
- **R4's lead time collapsed.** On runner days the median lead to +20% was 10 minutes, against 57 on train and 67 on
  validation. The population-wide hit rate held up (40.6%), but its false alarms are twice train's.

## Rule ideas this suggests (not tested here; train first, then an amendment)

1. A post-split watch, reverse split or ADS ratio change in the last 0-10 sessions: run the IP-T490 split-days panel
   by days since the split, split by float under 1M and foreign filer, scored on the full session rather than 09:45.
2. An all-day ignition alarm on the watchlist (10x the trailing 30-minute volume plus a session-high break) scored on
   the high-of-day timing. It must not be restricted to 06:00-09:10 or to the 5-15% gap band, because that is where
   R4 is blind.
3. Exhibit-99 text classification of 6-K and 8-K filings as a catalyst feature: about half of the "no news" runners
   are foreign filers whose only disclosure is a 6-K.
4. The day-2 fade (D+1 higher high only 13%; median D+2 -44% from the high) belongs to the fade-short work (IP-T479),
   with borrow measured by the IP-T480 pilot. It is not a long rule.

## How this was built

The `casestudy` stage in `tools/GapperStudy`, built into harness-bin15.

**Holdout guard.**

- The command refuses `--from` (or any `--extra` date) before 2026-09-10, and never requests a price, volume or minute
  bar dated before it.
- 2026-09-10 serves only as the first prior close, so the ranked sessions are 2026-09-11 .. 2026-11-09 (42 sessions).
- ADV and prior top-mover days use window sessions only (fewer in September).
- Event metadata (split dates and ratios, filing forms and dates, news headlines) is read up to 120 days back, only
  for the selected names, so OMH's 08-31 split can be dated. No holdout price or label is used.

**Selection.**

- Daily SIP bars, raw and split-adjusted, for every active Alpaca us_equity asset. Names delisted since 09-10 are not
  scanned.
- Gain is the daily high over the split-adjusted prior close. Filters: prior close $0.10-$20; ADV$ (window sessions)
  of $300k or more, or $1M or more traded that day; warrants dropped.
- A split on D comes from Alpaca's adjusted/raw ratio, which covers the ADS ratio changes Alpaca applied, such as
  NWGL 1:80.
- An unlisted gap of 2.5x or more is checked against corporate actions and split news, and re-based when confirmed.

**Catalyst.**

- Alpaca news from D-2 00:00 to D 20:00 ET. Market wraps that tag many tickers without naming the company are dropped,
  and reaction articles are used only as a last resort.
- EDGAR submissions with acceptance times from the filing headers.
- The catalyst is the earliest rated item between the prior close and the high. Failing that: the latest rated item
  before the prior close, then a rated filing, then the split (within 10 sessions), then other news, then none.

**Path.**

- 1-minute SIP bars 04:00-20:00 ET.
- Ignition is the first minute at or before the high with volume at least 10x the trailing 30-minute average
  (empty minutes count as zero), $25k or more traded, and a close above the session range and the prior close.
- The top footprint is VWAP (anchored at 04:00) lost after the high; the largest volume minute within 5 minutes of the
  high; a lower-high retest at 85-99% of the high after a 10% pullback; and halts within 15 minutes.
- D+1 and D+2 are put on D's share basis.

**R4.** As described in its section above. The reconstructed population differs from the study's in two ways: ADV
comes from window sessions only, and the frozen unexplained-move exclusion is not applied.
